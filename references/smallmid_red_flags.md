# Red Flags

Use this file when deciding whether a beaten-down stock is a mispricing candidate or a likely value trap.

## Hard Red Flags

These should usually force a `Pass` unless the user explicitly wants distressed or special-situation names:

- `going concern`
- `default`
- `delisting`
- `fraud`
- `restatement`
- `material weakness`
- `bankruptcy`
- repeated `NT 10-Q` or `NT 10-K`
- explicit near-term financing need with weak balance-sheet support

## Strong Negative Signals

These do not always force a `Pass`, but they should heavily reduce confidence:

- guidance cut
- secondary offering or strong dilution risk
- convert overhang or refinancing pressure
- covenant stress or debt maturity wall
- major customer concentration
- backlog deterioration
- inventory build that looks demand-related rather than strategic
- receivables stretch
- sudden executive departures tied to finance, accounting, or operations
- major litigation or regulatory overhang

## Structural Distortions And Special Situations

Treat these as a separate category rather than normal dislocation candidates:

- recent reverse split
- de-SPAC within the last `~24 months`
- strategic-review or busted-deal situations
- pre-revenue biotech
- pre-commercial medtech
- exchange-compliance notices short of formal delisting
- serial share-count growth that is changing the capital structure faster than the operating story

## Common Value-Trap Patterns

### The numbers look okay, but financing risk is rising

Examples:

- operating cash flow is barely positive
- cash balance is shrinking quickly
- debt is large relative to cash and current earnings power

### The earnings headline looks fine, but the quality is weak

Examples:

- revenue growth comes from acquisitions rather than organic demand
- margins are flattered by temporary cost cuts
- working capital is masking weak demand

### The business is too concentrated

Examples:

- one customer, one product line, or one channel explains too much of the story
- a contract reset or lost customer could change the whole thesis

### The market is reacting to dilution, not to operations

Examples:

- at-the-market issuance
- convert issuance
- equity raise after the selloff starts

### The chart looks washed out, but filings are stale or structurally weak

Examples:

- latest official filing set is too old to trust the current story
- cash flow looks positive mainly because working capital released cash
- near-term debt maturities or refinancing needs are doing more work than the earnings headline

## Recommended Classification

When red flags dominate, do not label the name as `overlooked`.
Use more precise language:

- `balance-sheet risk`
- `dilution overhang`
- `cyclical deterioration`
- `disclosure risk`
- `binary product or customer risk`
- `special situation or structural distortion`

If you cannot explain the selloff without hand-waving, lower the name to `Watchlist` or `Pass`.

## State Ceilings

Use these ceilings consistently:

- `hard red flag => Pass`
- `special situation => Pass` unless the user explicitly wants that category
- `stale filings, unresolved refinancing pressure, or meaningful dilution risk => max Watchlist`
- `Investigate` requires current filings, no hard flags, and a plausible non-terminal explanation for the selloff
